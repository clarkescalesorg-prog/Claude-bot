# Letter — Single Person Discount on Council Tax (with backdating)

Use when: you are the only adult (18+) living in your home, and either you've never claimed the 25% Single Person Discount, or your circumstances changed (partner moved out, children turned 18 and moved away, bereavement) and the discount wasn't applied.

Most councils have an online form — use it if available, but the *backdating request* below is what makes the difference. You're entitled to backdating to the date you actually became the only adult, up to 6 years (the limitation period for council tax overpayments).

---

## Content to use

> Subject: Single Person Discount — application and backdating request — Council Tax account [your account number]
>
> To [your council] Council Tax Team,
>
> I am writing to apply for the **25% Single Person Discount** on Council Tax for my property at the address below, and to **request backdating** to the date I first became the sole adult occupier.
>
> ### Account details
>
> - **Account number:** [your council tax account number, from your bill]
> - **Property address:** [full address with postcode]
> - **My name:** [full name]
> - **Date of birth:** [DD/MM/YYYY]
>
> ### Sole-adult occupier — confirmation
>
> I confirm that I am the only person aged 18 or over with sole or main residence at the above property. There are no other adults whose **main home** is this address.
>
> [If applicable, choose one or more:]
>
> - Other people resident at the address are aged under 18 (children: [number]).
> - [Name] is a full-time student in qualifying education at [institution] — [their main residence is term-time accommodation / they are disregarded under the student disregard rules].
> - [Name], previously resident, moved out on [date]. Their new permanent address is [address — if known].
> - [Name] passed away on [date]. The death certificate is enclosed / can be supplied.
>
> ### Backdating request
>
> I became the sole adult occupier on **[date]**. I therefore request that the discount be applied with effect from that date, and that any resulting overpayment be refunded.
>
> Under regulation 14 of the Council Tax (Administration and Enforcement) Regulations 1992, the council has discretion to backdate discounts where the applicant demonstrates that the criteria were met during the relevant period. The standard limitation period for council tax overpayments is 6 years.
>
> ### Evidence supporting the start date
>
> [Provide whatever applies — strongest evidence first:]
>
> - Council Tax bill from [previous council] confirming I left that property on [date].
> - Tenancy agreement / Land Registry title showing my move-in date of [date].
> - Royal Mail redirection records / DVLA address change / electoral roll entry.
> - Bank statements showing utility bills in my sole name from [date].
> - [Spouse/partner's]: new tenancy agreement / change-of-address letter / evidence of separation.
>
> ### Refund
>
> Please confirm:
> 1. The discount has been applied with effect from [date].
> 2. The overpayment has been calculated for each affected bill year.
> 3. The refund will be paid to my bank account (details on request) — **not** held as a credit against future bills, unless I expressly agree.
>
> Yours faithfully,
>
> [Full name]
> [Address]
> [Phone] · [Email]
> [Date]

---

## Tips

- If the council says they "only backdate 12 months as policy," ask them to point you to the **statutory basis** for that policy. There isn't one — it's a local discretion. Push back politely and reference the 6-year limitation period.
- If they refuse to backdate fully, request a written **decision notice** explaining their reasoning. You then have 2 months to appeal to the **Valuation Tribunal** (free, no lawyer needed).
- Don't forget to also register the address change with the Electoral Roll, DVLA, and HMRC — councils often cross-reference.
