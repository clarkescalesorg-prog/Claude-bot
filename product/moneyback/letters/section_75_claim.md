# Letter — Section 75 claim against credit card issuer

Use when: you paid for something costing **£100–£30,000** (in part or full) on a UK **credit card**, and the supplier failed to deliver, went bust, or the goods/services were faulty or misrepresented.

Send to: the credit card issuer's **disputes** address (search "[bank name] section 75 dispute address"). Most banks accept it via secure message in their app — that's faster and gives you a timestamped audit trail.

You do **not** need to wait for the merchant to refuse a refund. You can claim against the credit card issuer in parallel — and in some cases (merchant in administration), instead of.

---

## Content to use

> Subject: Section 75 claim — [merchant name] — transaction date [DD/MM/YYYY]
>
> To [Bank name] Disputes Team,
>
> I am making a claim under **Section 75 of the Consumer Credit Act 1974** in connection with the following credit card transaction:
>
> - **Cardholder:** [your name]
> - **Card number (last 4 digits):** [XXXX]
> - **Account/reference:** [if available]
> - **Merchant:** [merchant trading name and any company number]
> - **Transaction date:** [DD/MM/YYYY]
> - **Transaction amount:** £[amount]
> - **Total cost of goods/services:** £[total — note any portion paid by other means]
>
> ### What I purchased
>
> [Describe the goods or services in 2–3 sentences. Be factual. Include reference numbers, booking numbers, model numbers, etc.]
>
> ### What went wrong
>
> [Describe the breach in 4–8 sentences. Stick to the facts. Include dates of communication with the merchant. Choose the relevant category and adapt:]
>
> **Non-delivery:** *The goods/services were not delivered. The agreed delivery date was [date]. As of [today's date], no delivery has occurred and the merchant has not provided a revised date. I have contacted the merchant on [dates] and [summarise their response or non-response].*
>
> **Faulty / not as described:** *The goods/services were materially different from what was advertised. Specifically, [...]. This is a breach of the Consumer Rights Act 2015 (sections 9, 10, and 11 — satisfactory quality, fit for purpose, as described). I requested a remedy from the merchant on [date]; they [refused / failed to respond / offered an inadequate remedy of ...].*
>
> **Merchant insolvency:** *The merchant has entered [administration / liquidation / dissolution] on [date], as confirmed by [Companies House / administrator's statement / press notice]. The goods/services I paid for will not be delivered.*
>
> ### Evidence enclosed
>
> - Receipt / order confirmation
> - Card statement extract showing the transaction
> - All correspondence with the merchant ([n] emails / messages)
> - [Photos / screenshots / expert reports / insolvency notice — as applicable]
>
> ### Remedy requested
>
> Under Section 75, [Bank name] is **jointly and severally liable with the merchant** for any breach of contract or misrepresentation. I therefore request a full refund of:
>
> - The transaction amount: **£[amount]**
> - Plus consequential losses arising from the breach: **£[amount]** [only include if you have receipts — e.g. cost of replacement holiday, urgent alternative service]
> - **Total claim: £[total]**
>
> Please credit this amount to the card account.
>
> ### Timescale
>
> I expect a substantive response within **8 weeks** (FCA DISP rules). If the claim is not resolved by then, or if the response rejects the claim, I will refer the matter to the **Financial Ombudsman Service** without further notice.
>
> Yours faithfully,
>
> [Full name]
> [Address]
> [Phone] · [Email]
> [Date]

## Tips

- **Don't accept "we'll do a chargeback instead"** as a substitute. Chargeback is a card-scheme courtesy, time-limited (~120 days), and not a legal right. Section 75 is statutory and unlimited in time. Insist they raise it as a Section 75 claim.
- **The £100 minimum is per item, not per transaction.** If you bought a £200 item and a £50 item in one transaction, only the £200 item is Section 75-eligible. (But if the *single item's price* was £100+ and you paid only a £10 deposit on the card and the rest by bank transfer, the *whole* item is still covered.)
- **Joint liability means**: even if the merchant is solvent and refusing, the card issuer must refund you and pursue the merchant themselves. Don't let them tell you to "go back to the merchant."
- **Financial Ombudsman Service** is free. Banks pay £750 per case referred regardless of outcome, so they have a strong incentive to settle reasonable claims at the 8-week mark.
- **Backdate:** there's no time limit in Section 75 itself, but the standard FOS limitation is 6 years from the breach (or 3 years from when you knew or could have known about the loss).
