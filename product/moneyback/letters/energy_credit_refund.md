# Letter — Energy supplier credit balance refund

Use when: your gas/electricity account has been in credit (you've paid more by direct debit than you've used) for any meaningful length of time. Under Ofgem's standard licence conditions, suppliers must return credit on request once it's clearly more than reasonable — there's no "we keep your money" policy that's actually compliant.

Send via: your supplier's app/portal as a secure message **and** by email to their complaints address. The dual-track creates a paper trail if you need to escalate to the Energy Ombudsman.

---

## Step 1 — Submit a meter reading first

Before requesting the refund, take fresh meter readings (gas + electricity) and submit them via the app. If your account is on **estimated** usage, the credit balance might be artificially inflated (or, less commonly, the bill is under-stated). A real reading is the basis of the refund calculation.

For smart meters: confirm in the app that recent readings are showing — if they're missing, your supplier may be running on estimates anyway.

---

## Step 2 — Letter

> Subject: Refund of credit balance — account [your account number]
>
> To [Supplier name] customer relations,
>
> I am writing to request a refund of the credit balance currently held on my energy account.
>
> ### Account details
>
> - **Account holder:** [your full name]
> - **Account number:** [number, from your bill]
> - **Supply address:** [full address with postcode]
> - **MPAN (electricity):** [13-digit, from bill]
> - **MPRN (gas):** [10-digit, from bill]
>
> ### Current position
>
> - **Latest meter readings submitted:** electricity [reading] on [date]; gas [reading] on [date].
> - **Current credit balance after these readings:** £[amount] (per the app/portal as of [date]).
> - **Monthly direct debit:** £[amount].
> - **Months in credit:** [n] consecutive months.
>
> ### Request
>
> Please refund the full credit balance to my bank account.
>
> Under Ofgem's Standard Licence Condition 27 and your Standards of Conduct obligations, suppliers must promptly return customer credit on request where the balance materially exceeds the next month's billing.
>
> [Optional — only if you don't want to renegotiate the DD:]
>
> Please retain my current direct debit at £[amount]. I do not wish to alter it as part of this refund.
>
> [Optional — if you want to lower the DD:]
>
> Please also reduce my monthly direct debit to a level appropriate for my actual usage based on the latest readings. My calculated annual usage is approximately [amount] kWh electricity and [amount] kWh gas (per Ofgem's typical-domestic-consumption figures or your own consumption history).
>
> ### Bank details
>
> Account name: [name]
> Sort code: [XX-XX-XX]
> Account number: [XXXXXXXX]
>
> ### Timescale
>
> I expect the refund to be processed within **10 working days** in line with industry standards. If it is not paid by [today + 10 working days], I will treat this as a formal complaint and will escalate to the **Energy Ombudsman** after the 8-week regulatory deadline.
>
> Yours faithfully,
>
> [Full name]
> [Contact phone]
> [Contact email]
> [Date]

## Tips

- **Don't accept a credit reduction in lieu of cash.** Suppliers will sometimes say "we'll credit it forward against winter usage." Refuse politely: under Ofgem's rules you have the right to a cash refund.
- **Lower the direct debit too if possible.** A correct DD should clear your annual usage with no large credit balance accumulating. If your DD is set higher than needed, the supplier benefits from interest-free working capital — yours.
- **For old accounts:** if you switched supplier in the last 6 years and there was credit on the closing bill, phone the **old** supplier. Ofgem requires suppliers to issue final bills within 6 weeks of switch and to refund any credit balance. They keep records for 6 years. Quote the date of switch and the property address.
- **Energy Ombudsman:** free service. After 8 weeks (or after a "deadlock letter" from the supplier), submit at energyombudsman.org. The supplier pays £400+ in case fees regardless of outcome, so the threat alone usually gets reasonable cases settled.
- **Estimated bills causing the credit:** if the credit is large and your account has been on estimates, request a **rebill** based on actual readings *and* the refund. The rebill could reduce the credit but it could also unlock a refund of overpaid VAT (5% on energy).
