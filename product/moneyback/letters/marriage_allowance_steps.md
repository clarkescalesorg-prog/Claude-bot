# Marriage Allowance — step-by-step (no letter needed)

Marriage Allowance is the only item in this pack where you don't need a letter at all — HMRC has a 5-minute online form. This is here to walk you through the form so you fill it in correctly and don't lose backdated years.

**Worth:** £252 per tax year, backdatable up to 4 years. Total: up to ~£1,260.

---

## 1. Are you eligible?

You qualify if **all** of these are true for the tax year you're claiming:

- You are married or in a civil partnership.
- The **lower earner** has total taxable income below £12,570 (the personal allowance). This includes pensions, savings interest, rental income — not just wages.
- The **higher earner** has total taxable income between £12,571 and £50,270 (basic-rate band). Higher-rate or additional-rate taxpayers cannot receive the transfer.
- Both partners were born on or after 6 April 1935. (If born before, claim the older "Married Couple's Allowance" instead — different scheme, more generous.)

**Edge cases:**
- Maternity leave, sabbatical, taking a year out: still eligible if total annual income falls under the threshold.
- Self-employed with low profits: still eligible if profits + other income < £12,570.
- Recently bereaved: you can still claim for any tax year your spouse was alive and the criteria were met. Phone HMRC; the online form doesn't handle this.

---

## 2. Who applies?

The **lower earner** applies. They are the one transferring 10% of their personal allowance to their partner. This matters because the form is in *their* name and uses *their* Government Gateway login.

Common mistake: the higher earner trying to apply. The form will reject this.

---

## 3. What you need before you start

- Both partners' **National Insurance numbers**.
- The lower earner's Government Gateway user ID and password (set one up at gov.uk/log-in-register-hmrc-online-services if you don't have one — bring photo ID and a recent payslip or P60).
- Approximate income figures for each year you want to claim (2021–22, 2022–23, 2023–24, 2024–25, plus the current year).

---

## 4. Apply

Go to: **gov.uk/marriage-allowance/how-to-apply**

The form asks:

1. Your details (the lower earner's NI number and date of birth).
2. Your spouse/partner's details (NI number, date of birth, full name as on their payslips/P60).
3. Confirmation of the marriage/civil partnership date.
4. **Tax years to apply.** Tick **all four backdate years** plus the current year. This is the bit people miss — if you only tick the current year, you forfeit ~£1,000 of refund.
5. Confirm the lower earner's income was below the personal allowance for each ticked year.

Submit. You'll see a reference number — save it.

---

## 5. What happens next

- **Current year:** the higher earner's tax code changes (typically to 1383M if the standard 1257L applied before). Their take-home goes up by ~£21/month within 1–2 pay cycles.
- **Backdated years:** HMRC issues a P800 calculation for each year, then either:
  - A **cheque** posted to the higher earner (allow 6–10 weeks), or
  - A **bank transfer** if HMRC has bank details (faster).

If you don't see anything within 12 weeks, phone 0300 200 3300 quoting your reference.

---

## 6. Don't pay a refund company

You'll see Google ads for "Marriage Tax Refund" or "Marriage Allowance Claims" companies that promise to do this for you. They use the exact same gov.uk form and take 30–42% of the refund, plus an admin fee. The form takes 5 minutes. Do it yourself.

If you've already used one of these companies and want to cancel:
- They are bound by the **Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013** — 14-day cooling-off period.
- After 14 days, you can still cancel, but they may take a fee for "work done so far."
- HMRC has been issuing refunds **directly to the taxpayer** (not the agent) since 2022 to limit these companies' grip — phone 0300 200 3300 and ask for the agent's authority to be revoked from your account.

---

## 7. After you've claimed

- Set a calendar reminder at the start of each new tax year (April).
- If the lower earner's income rises **above** £12,570, the allowance no longer benefits you and may even cost you tax. Cancel by phoning HMRC or via your Personal Tax Account.
- If the higher earner's income rises **above** £50,270 into higher-rate, the transfer must stop — HMRC will usually catch this and adjust, but verify on your next P60.
