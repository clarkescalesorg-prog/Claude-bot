# The UK Money-Back Pack

**18 things UK households are leaving on the table — and exactly how to claim them back.**

---

## What this is

A short, no-fluff guide to the rebates, allowances, tax reliefs, and refund routes that most UK households are entitled to but don't claim. Every item below is a real government, regulator, or statutory scheme. Read the section, do the action, get the money.

If you only do three things from this guide — challenge your council tax band, claim Marriage Allowance, and check Pension Credit for any older relative — you'll likely have made the £19 cost back several times over.

**How to use:** open the eligibility checker (in the bundle, or run online at `/check` on the storefront), answer 10 questions, and it'll flag the 3–5 sections most worth your time. Or just skim the headings — each section has a "Who it's for" line at the top.

> **Important:** rates and thresholds shown are accurate at time of writing. Tax year and benefit rates change every April. The gov.uk page linked in each section is always authoritative — check it before you submit.

## §1 — Council Tax band challenge

**Who:** anyone in England or Scotland whose home was valued in 1991 (or 2003 for Wales). That's most homes.
**What it's worth:** average successful challenge: £400–£1,500 refund of overpaid tax (backdated to when you moved in), plus £100–£400/year ongoing.

The 1991 valuation was rushed — second-job estate agents drove past streets and assigned bands in minutes. An estimated 400,000 English homes are in the wrong band.

**The two-test method:**

1. **Neighbour check.** Find your address at gov.uk/council-tax-bands. Look up 4–6 nearby homes of similar size and type. If half of them are in a lower band than yours, you have a case.
2. **1991 value check.** Use a historical price calculator (e.g. Nationwide's House Price Index back-calculator) to estimate what your home was worth on 1 April 1991. Compare to the band thresholds:

| Band | 1991 value (England) |
|------|----------------------|
| A    | up to £40,000        |
| B    | £40,001–£52,000      |
| C    | £52,001–£68,000      |
| D    | £68,001–£88,000      |
| E    | £88,001–£120,000     |
| F    | £120,001–£160,000    |
| G    | £160,001–£320,000    |
| H    | £320,001+            |

If **both** tests suggest your band is too high, request a review with the Valuation Office Agency (VOA) — see the letter template in `letters/council_tax_band_challenge.md`. They'll review for free.

**Risk warning:** the VOA can also move neighbouring properties up if they discover the whole street is mis-banded low. Always do the neighbour check first. If your neighbours are in the same band as you, don't challenge.

**Action:** gov.uk/challenge-council-tax-band

## §2 — Single Person Discount (council tax)

**Who:** anyone who's the only adult (18+) in their home.
**What it's worth:** 25% off your council tax bill. For a Band D household that's typically £450–£600/year.

Often missed by:
- Newly separated/divorced people (council tax doesn't auto-update when a spouse moves out).
- People whose adult children have moved out for university or work.
- Widowed pensioners.

**Action:** apply directly through your local council's website. Search "[your council] single person discount". Backdate is usually allowed up to 6 years — ask explicitly.

## §3 — Severe Mental Impairment (SMI) council tax exemption

**Who:** households where someone is medically diagnosed with a severe mental impairment (dementia, severe learning disability, stroke causing cognitive impairment, Parkinson's with cognitive symptoms, etc.) **and** receives a qualifying benefit (Attendance Allowance, PIP daily-living, ESA, etc.).
**What it's worth:** if the SMI person lives with one other adult — 25% off. If they live alone or only with other SMI/exempt people — 100% off. Backdating to the date of diagnosis is common: refunds of £5,000–£15,000 are routinely granted.

This is one of the most under-claimed reliefs in the UK. Councils are not legally required to publicise it.

**Action:**
1. Get a GP to sign the council's SMI certificate (free).
2. Confirm the qualifying benefit is in payment.
3. Apply to your council with both. **Ask in writing for full backdating to the diagnosis date.**

## §4 — Marriage Allowance

**Who:** married or civil-partnered couples where one partner earns under £12,570 (the personal allowance) and the other earns between £12,571 and £50,270 (basic-rate band).
**What it's worth:** £252 per tax year, backdatable 4 years. Total: up to ~£1,260.

The lower earner transfers 10% of their unused personal allowance to the higher earner. HMRC then issues a tax-code adjustment or a cheque for previous years.

**Action:** the lower earner applies at gov.uk/marriage-allowance. Takes 5 minutes. Ask for the full 4-year backdate. Don't use third-party "claims companies" — they take 40% for filling in the same form you can do for free.

**Common mistake:** people stop the allowance when one partner gets a pay rise into the higher-rate band. You need to actively cancel it; HMRC won't always notice for a year.

## §5 — Working from Home tax relief

**Who:** anyone required by their employer to work from home for any part of a tax year (including before 2022 — the rules were looser).
**What it's worth:** £62.40/year at basic rate, £124.80 at higher rate. Backdatable 4 years if you didn't claim during COVID. Total: up to ~£500 at higher rate.

**Action:** gov.uk/tax-relief-for-employees/working-at-home — there's a "microservice" form. You'll need a Government Gateway login (set one up if you don't have one — useful for many of the items in this guide).

**Note:** the rules tightened from April 2022 — you can no longer claim if you chose to work from home. You can still backclaim 2020–21 and 2021–22 if you didn't already.

## §6 — Uniform / tools tax relief

**Who:** anyone who wears a branded uniform to work, or buys their own tools or specialist clothing (nurses, mechanics, builders, chefs, hairdressers, joiners, electricians).
**What it's worth:** flat-rate allowances by trade — £60 to £140/year, backdatable 4 years. Total: up to ~£700 at higher rate, ~£280 at basic.

**Trade-specific rates** (a sample): nurses £125, hairdressers £60, joiners £140, mechanics £120.

**Action:** gov.uk/tax-relief-for-employees/uniforms-work-clothing-and-tools. Form P87 if claiming over £1,000 of expenses; otherwise online.

**Don't pay a refund company** — they'll take 30–50%. The form takes 10 minutes.

## §7 — Higher-rate pension tax relief top-up

**Who:** anyone earning over £50,270 who pays into a personal pension (SIPP, stakeholder, or workplace scheme using "relief at source").
**What it's worth:** an extra 20% on top of the 20% your provider claims automatically. On £5,000 of contributions, that's £1,000 you have to actively reclaim.

If you're in a "salary-sacrifice" workplace pension, this doesn't apply — relief is already given through PAYE. Check your payslip — if your pension contribution comes off **before** tax is calculated, it's salary-sacrifice and you're sorted.

**Action:**
- If you do a Self Assessment, declare contributions on the pension page.
- If you don't, write to HMRC (or call 0300 200 3300) with: contribution amount, scheme name, tax year. Backdatable 4 years.

## §8 — Tax-Free Childcare

**Who:** working parents (each earning at least minimum wage × 16 hours, neither earning over £100,000) of children under 12 (or under 17 if disabled).
**What it's worth:** the government tops up your childcare account by 25% — up to £2,000/year per child (£4,000 for disabled).

**Compatible with:** the 15/30 free hours scheme (§9), but **not** with childcare vouchers (you'll be moved off those if you claim).

**Action:** gov.uk/tax-free-childcare. Set up a childcare account, pay your provider through it.

**Catch:** you need to "reconfirm" eligibility every 3 months. Set a calendar reminder — accounts get suspended quietly.

## §9 — Free childcare hours (England)

**Who:** working parents of children aged 9 months to 4 years (eligibility rolling out through 2024–25; check current ages on gov.uk).
**What it's worth:** 15 or 30 hours per week of free childcare, term-time (38 weeks). At average UK childcare rates (£7/hour), that's £4,000–£8,000/year per child.

**Income limit:** each parent earns under £100,000.

**Action:** apply at gov.uk/30-hours-free-childcare for an "eligibility code" before the term you want it to start. Then give the code to your nursery.

## §10 — Pension Credit

**Who:** anyone over State Pension age with a weekly income under ~£218 (single) or ~£333 (couple).
**What it's worth:** average award £3,900/year — and crucially, it's a **gateway** to free TV licence, council tax reduction, Warm Home Discount, Cold Weather Payment, and (depending on the year) cost-of-living payments. Total real value often £5,000+.

**~850,000 eligible UK pensioners do not claim it.** This is the single biggest under-claimed benefit in the country.

**Action:**
- Use the calculator at gov.uk/pension-credit-calculator.
- Apply by phone (0800 99 1234) — much easier than online for older claimants.
- **Apply even if you think you're £5/week over** — savings credit and additional amounts (carer, severe disability) can lift you back into eligibility.

If you have an older parent or relative, do this for them. Today.

## §11 — Attendance Allowance

**Who:** anyone over State Pension age who needs help with daily tasks because of a physical or mental health condition. **You don't need to actually receive that help** — it's about needing it.
**What it's worth:** £73.90/week (lower) or £110.40/week (higher) for 2025–26. Annually: £3,840–£5,740. Tax-free, not means-tested.

You don't have to be physically disabled. Eligible conditions include arthritis, heart disease, COPD, dementia, partial sight, severe anxiety, mobility issues from any cause.

**Action:** request form AA1 from 0800 731 0122 or download from gov.uk/attendance-allowance/how-to-claim. The form is long. Get a Citizens Advice / Age UK adviser to help fill it in — their success rate is materially higher than self-completed forms.

**Often unlocks:** Carer's Allowance for the person caring for the claimant (§13).

## §12 — Personal Independence Payment (PIP)

**Who:** anyone aged 16 to State Pension age with a long-term health condition affecting daily living or mobility.
**What it's worth:** £29.20/week to £187.45/week (2025–26 standard rates). Annually: £1,500–£9,750. Tax-free, not means-tested.

PIP is replacing DLA for working-age adults. Conditions include — but are not limited to — anxiety/depression, autism, ADHD, chronic pain, diabetes, MS, Crohn's, long COVID, fibromyalgia, hearing/sight loss.

**Action:** apply at gov.uk/pip/how-to-claim. Phone application (0800 917 2222) is faster than online. Be specific about *bad days*, not average days. Get help from Citizens Advice or a disability charity for the form — DWP refusal rates fall by 50%+ with assisted applications.

**Common mistake:** people don't claim because they think they're "not disabled enough." PIP is awarded on functional impact, not diagnosis severity.

## §13 — Carer's Allowance

**Who:** anyone caring for someone 35+ hours per week who receives PIP (daily living component) or Attendance Allowance, where the carer earns under £196/week after deductions.
**What it's worth:** £83.30/week (2025–26) — roughly £4,330/year.

**Action:** gov.uk/carers-allowance. Apply online with the cared-for person's NI number.

**Watch-out:** Carer's Allowance counts as income for the person being cared for if they get a "severe disability premium" as part of Pension Credit — claiming it can sometimes reduce their other benefits. Run both numbers through a benefits calculator (entitledto.co.uk or turn2us.org.uk) before claiming.

## §14 — Warm Home Discount

**Who:** people on Pension Credit (Guarantee element), or on means-tested benefits with high energy costs (the "broader group" — eligibility set by your supplier).
**What it's worth:** £150 credit on your electricity bill, applied automatically each winter for "core group" (Pension Credit) recipients.

**Action:**
- Core group: nothing — it's automatic if you receive Pension Credit Guarantee Credit on the qualifying date (early August). If you don't see the credit by January, phone the DWP Warm Home Discount helpline (0800 731 0214).
- Broader group: apply directly with your energy supplier. **Limited spaces** — apply early (October–November).

## §15 — Cold Weather Payment

**Who:** people on Pension Credit, Income Support, JSA, ESA, or Universal Credit with certain elements.
**What it's worth:** £25 for each 7-day period where the average temperature in your postcode area is at or below 0°C.

**Action:** automatic. If you're eligible and your postcode triggers a payment, it lands within 14 days. Check status at gov.uk/cold-weather-payment.

## §16 — Section 75 chargeback (credit card disputes)

**Who:** anyone who paid for goods or services costing **between £100 and £30,000** at least partly on a UK **credit card**, where the supplier failed to deliver, went bust, or sold something faulty/misrepresented.
**What it's worth:** the full amount of the failed transaction — sometimes including consequential losses (e.g. cost of a replacement holiday).

Section 75 of the Consumer Credit Act 1974 makes the credit card company **jointly liable** with the merchant. Most people don't know this.

**Common winning cases:**
- Holiday/flight bookings cancelled with no refund.
- Wedding venues that went insolvent.
- Builders or kitchen fitters who took deposits and disappeared.
- Faulty furniture or electronics where the retailer refuses a refund.
- Concert/festival tickets where the event was cancelled and the agent won't refund.

**Critical detail:** Section 75 only applies to credit cards (not debit, not BNPL). For debit cards / BNPL, request a "chargeback" instead — softer and time-limited (usually 120 days from the disputed transaction).

**Action:** see `letters/section_75_claim.md`. Send to the credit card issuer's disputes address. They have 8 weeks to respond before you can escalate to the Financial Ombudsman.

## §17 — Energy supplier credit balance refunds

**Who:** anyone whose energy account has been in credit (i.e. you've paid more by direct debit than you've used) for 3+ months.
**What it's worth:** the full credit balance — typically £150–£800 for a household that's overpaid through a winter. Suppliers are obliged under Ofgem rules to refund credit on request once it exceeds one month's payment.

**Action:** log in to your supplier's app/website, submit a meter reading, then **call** and request a refund of the credit balance. Don't accept "we'll spread it over the year" — request the cash refund. They have 10 working days to pay it.

**For accounts you've left:** if you switched supplier in the last 6 years and there was a credit balance, it may have been written off. Phone the **old** supplier and request the final account statement and any credit refund. They are obliged to keep records and pay out.

## §18 — Mis-sold packaged bank account refunds

**Who:** anyone who's been paying £10–£25/month for a "packaged" current account (bundling travel insurance, breakdown cover, mobile insurance, etc.) where one or more of the bundled features was useless to them — e.g. travel insurance you couldn't use due to age or health, or breakdown cover when you don't drive.
**What it's worth:** typically a refund of all monthly fees (£500–£3,000+ over the years held), plus 8% statutory interest.

Banks were fined heavily in 2013–2018 for mis-selling these. Many customers were never properly assessed for whether they could use the bundled products.

**Action:**
1. Write to your bank's customer relations using the FOS template ("I believe my packaged account was mis-sold because...").
2. If they reject or offer a partial refund, escalate to the Financial Ombudsman Service within 6 months — free, independent, often increases the offer.

## How to track your claims

Open `claims_tracker.csv` (in this bundle). It has columns:

```
Item | Date submitted | Reference no. | Expected response by | Amount claimed | Status | Notes
```

Discipline matters. Most claims are paid within 4–8 weeks. If you don't hear back by the expected date, chase. The standard escalation route is:

1. Original department (4 weeks).
2. Formal complaint within the same organisation (8 weeks).
3. Independent ombudsman (Financial Ombudsman, Local Government Ombudsman, HMRC adjudicator, etc.) — free, binding on the organisation.

## What's *not* in this guide (and why)

- **PPI claims** — the deadline passed in 2019. Anything you see advertised today is recycled noise.
- **"HMRC tax refund" cold callers** — these are scams. HMRC will never text or phone you to offer a refund.
- **No-win-no-fee claim companies** — for everything in this guide, doing it yourself is straightforward and avoids losing 30–50% to fees.
- **Universal Credit / Housing Benefit** — these are means-tested benefits, not "money back." Use entitledto.co.uk or turn2us.org.uk for a full benefits check.
